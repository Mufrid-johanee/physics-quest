## Floor 1 — Current & Charge

### NPC: Asha
**Role:** Electrical Laboratory Instructor  
**Personality:** Friendly, energetic, clear and encouraging  
**Teaching style:** Demonstration → observation → basic reasoning

### Pre-Quiz Dialogue

**Asha:**  
"Welcome to the electrical lab. Before we connect anything, let's start with the simplest question: what actually moves through a circuit?"

**Player:**  
"Electric current?"

**Asha:**  
"Correct. But don't think of current as something that simply appears when we connect a wire. It depends on the movement of electric charge."

**Player:**  
"So the circuit has to provide a path for the charge?"

**Asha:**  
"Exactly. A complete path allows charge to move through the circuit."

**Player:**  
"And if the path is broken?"

**Asha:**  
"Then the current cannot continue through that path."

**Player:**  
"Got it. A complete circuit gives the charge a continuous path."

**Asha:**  
"Good. Now let's see whether you can identify what happens when that path changes."

**Asha:**  
"Your first challenge is ready."

## Floor 2 — Voltage, Resistance & Ohm's Law

### NPC: Imran
**Role:** Circuit Analysis Technician  
**Personality:** Precise, patient, analytical  
**Teaching style:** Measure → compare → calculate

### Pre-Quiz Dialogue

**Imran:**  
"Before you touch the circuit, look at the readings."

**Player:**  
"The voltage and current are different."

**Imran:**  
"They measure different things. Voltage describes the potential difference that drives charge through a circuit, while current describes the rate of charge flow."

**Player:**  
"Then where does resistance fit in?"

**Imran:**  
"Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes the flow of current."

**Player:**  
"So changing the resistance can change the current?"

**Imran:**  
"Yes. For a simple ohmic relationship, voltage, current, and resistance are connected."

**Player:**  
"That's Ohm's law."

**Imran:**  
"Exactly. But memorizing the equation is not enough. You need to understand what happens when one quantity changes."

**Player:**  
"Then I'll check the other quantities before deciding what happens."

**Imran:**  
"That's the habit of a good circuit analyst. Your measurements are waiting."

## Floor 3 — Series & Parallel Circuits

### NPC: Rafi
**Role:** Electrical Systems Instructor  
**Personality:** Practical, confident, slightly playful  
**Teaching style:** Compare → predict → verify

### Pre-Quiz Dialogue

**Rafi:**  
"Here's a circuit. Now here's another one. They contain similar components, but their connections are different."

**Player:**  
"One looks like a single path, while the other splits into branches."

**Rafi:**  
"Exactly. And that difference matters."

**Player:**  
"The first is a series circuit, and the second is parallel?"

**Rafi:**  
"Correct. In a series arrangement, components share the same path. In a parallel arrangement, current has multiple branches available."

**Player:**  
"So the current behaves differently in each arrangement."

**Rafi:**  
"Yes. And the way voltage is distributed also depends on the arrangement."

**Player:**  
"Can I predict the result before measuring it?"

**Rafi:**  
"That is exactly what I want you to do."

**Player:**  
"Then I'll make a prediction first and use the measurements to check it."

**Rafi:**  
"Perfect. Don't let the circuit surprise you. Make it prove you right—or prove you wrong."

## Floor 4 — Magnetic Fields & Electromagnetism

### NPC: Dr. Samira
**Role:** Electromagnetism Researcher  
**Personality:** Calm, curious, observant  
**Teaching style:** Observe → identify pattern → explain

### Pre-Quiz Dialogue

**Dr. Samira:**  
"Take a look at the equipment. What do you notice around the current-carrying wire?"

**Player:**  
"There is a magnetic effect around it."

**Dr. Samira:**  
"Good. Electric current can produce a magnetic field."

**Player:**  
"So electricity and magnetism are connected."

**Dr. Samira:**  
"Very closely. That connection is one of the foundations of electromagnetism."

**Player:**  
"Can the direction of the magnetic field change?"

**Dr. Samira:**  
"Yes. Its direction is related to the direction of the current. The geometry of the field matters too."

**Player:**  
"So I shouldn't just memorize a diagram."

**Dr. Samira:**  
"Exactly. Observe the direction, identify the pattern, and then explain why it occurs."

**Player:**  
"Then I'll look for the pattern before choosing an answer."

**Dr. Samira:**  
"Good. The field map is ready. Let's see what you can discover."

## Floor 5 — Electromagnetic Applications & Integrated Circuit Reasoning

### NPC: Arman
**Role:** Electrical Systems Engineer  
**Personality:** Focused, resourceful, supportive  
**Teaching style:** Diagnose → connect concepts → solve

### Pre-Quiz Dialogue

**Arman:**  
"You've learned the individual pieces. Now I want you to use them together."

**Player:**  
"So this floor is more like a complete problem?"

**Arman:**  
"Exactly. Real electrical systems rarely present one isolated concept at a time."

**Player:**  
"What should I check first?"

**Arman:**  
"Start with the evidence. What is connected? What is being measured? What has changed?"

**Player:**  
"Then I identify the relevant electrical principle."

**Arman:**  
"Good. After that, predict the result before you change anything."

**Player:**  
"And then verify the prediction with the circuit."

**Arman:**  
"That's the process."

**Player:**  
"So the goal isn't just to remember formulas. It's to use the concepts to diagnose the system."

**Arman:**  
"Exactly. You've reached the point where the circuit should become a problem you can reason through."

**Player:**  
"Let's test it."

**Arman:**  
"Your final floor challenge begins now."

# Dialogue Design Rules

## 1. One primary NPC per floor
The player should associate each floor's central lesson with its assigned character.

## 2. Volt is supplementary
Volt should not introduce or replace the core lesson. Its main purposes are optional hints, short environmental interactions, light humor, and reinforcement.

## 3. Player participation
Every pre-quiz conversation should give the player at least one opportunity to observe something, identify a concept, make a prediction, explain a relationship, or choose a reasoning path.

## 4. Avoid lecture dumps
The NPC should not explain the entire floor before the quiz. The dialogue establishes the concept and prepares the player for the challenge.

## 5. Progression across the five floors

**Floor 1:** What is happening?

**Floor 2:** What can I measure?

**Floor 3:** What changes when the arrangement changes?

**Floor 4:** What pattern can I observe?

**Floor 5:** How can I use everything I know to solve the problem?
